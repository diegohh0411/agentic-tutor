# Local STT, VAD, and turn detection for a macOS voice tutor

**Research date:** 2026-10-21  
**Target:** entirely local inference; minimum M1 Pro / 16 GB unified memory; comfortable M3/M4 Pro / 32 GB+.  
**Scope:** speech recognition (streaming and batch), speech activity detection, and end-of-turn/semantic endpointing.  
**Evidence note:** throughput figures below are not cross-comparable unless hardware, pipeline and corpus match. I label upstream vendor measurements, third-party measurements and missing data. RTFx > 1 means faster than real time; RTF < 1 means slower than real time. Latency and perceived interruption behavior matter more than batch RTF for a tutor.

## Executive assessment

For an on-device MVP, **FluidAudio’s Parakeet Core ML** is the strongest production-shaped ASR option when its 25 European languages cover the user; it is native Swift/Core ML and its published M4 Pro/M5 results are extremely fast. It does not cover Mexican Spanish specifically as a distinct variety or many languages outside its fixed list, so keep **WhisperKit / Whisper large-v3-turbo** as the broad-language fallback. For a first interaction, batch/short-utterance transcription is simpler and often more accurate; for live captions, use a streaming recognizer such as **Parakeet Unified** (English) or test **Voxtral Mini 4B Realtime** on 32 GB machines. Treat advertised “streaming” as a latency/quality tradeoff, not equivalent to incremental low-latency conversation: Whisper-family models are fundamentally chunked/offline.

For endpointing, pair a frame-level VAD with semantic detection. **TEN VAD** is a compelling low-latency candidate with a native macOS arm64 library and published M1 measurements; **Silero VAD v6 ONNX** is the licensing/integration fallback. Run **Pipecat Smart Turn v3.x** only at pauses after VAD: it is tiny, multilingual and ONNX-friendly. Its classification is language understanding of turn completion, not ASR. LiveKit’s text-context detector is a useful alternative if its model license and transcript dependence fit.

## Benchmarks and evaluation cautions

- The [Open ASR Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/huggingface.co/spaces/open-asr-leaderboard/leaderboard) is a useful standardized reference, but its 2025 leaderboard analysis warns that most short-form English results do not predict multilingual or long-form performance. Prefer its per-dataset results over a single blended rank. Cohere’s March 2026 release is a newer addition and claims #1 English placement.
- The [FluidAudio benchmark page](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio/blob/main/Documentation/Benchmarks.md) is unusually useful because it publishes Apple hardware, model build/runtime, corpus and RTFx. It reports M4 Pro Parakeet TDT and M5 Pro streaming tiers; it does **not** claim those numbers for M1 Pro.
- WER numbers from LibriSpeech clean, LibriSpeech other, FLEURS and Open ASR are not interchangeable. Accent, microphone distance, room noise, code-switching, tutoring vocabulary, and normalization can dominate ranking. Mexican Spanish should be separately evaluated with Mexican-accent speech and proper nouns.
- For memory, published peaks are scarce. Weight size is a lower bound, not total residency: Core ML compilation/cache, activations, audio buffers, and the tutor LLM also consume unified memory. Leave enough headroom for the LLM and UI; do not choose an ASR solely because weights fit.
- I searched current HF model cards, leaderboard material, official project documentation/GitHub and release posts, including recent 2026 releases. X/Twitter search was not available as a reliable source in this research pass; I have not treated social posts as benchmark evidence.

## MVP recommendation

### STT

**Primary: FluidAudio Parakeet TDT v3 Core ML** for supported European languages, with a locally measured confidence gate and short utterance chunks. Reasons: Swift-native integration, Neural Engine use, excellent published M4 Pro throughput/LibriSpeech accuracy, and much lower weight footprint than large Whisper or large decoder models. For English live streaming, evaluate **Parakeet Unified Core ML** specifically; its streaming path has tested chunk tiers and one Swift runtime.

**Fallback: WhisperKit with large-v3-turbo.** It offers wide language coverage, mature Swift integration, and MIT weights/runtime. It is chunked rather than truly causal; use incremental overlapping windows or wait for VAD endpoint, and surface revisions carefully. On 16 GB M1 Pro, begin with turbo/quantized settings and benchmark peak unified memory alongside the tutor model. For broad languages it is a safer fallback than forcing Parakeet’s fixed 25-language list.

**Stretch candidate:** Voxtral Mini 4B Realtime or Kyutai STT 1B when genuine streaming latency is the product requirement. Neither has sufficient verified Mac performance evidence yet for the baseline recommendation.

### VAD

**Primary: TEN VAD** (subject to license review), using its native macOS arm64 C framework; it has published M1 timing and targets fast conversational onset/offset.  
**Fallback: Silero VAD v6 ONNX**, MIT licensed, well-used, lightweight, straightforward through ONNX Runtime C API. Tune threshold, hangover and minimum silence per accessibility settings rather than hard-coding one latency profile.

### Turn detection

**Primary: Pipecat Smart Turn v3.x**, called only after the VAD signals a pause. At ~8M parameters and 8 MB int8 it adds semantic “finished vs hesitating” evidence at low resource cost and supports a multilingual workflow. Integrate ONNX Runtime first; evaluate the third-party Core ML conversion only if you need all-native deployment.

**Fallback: LiveKit multilingual turn detector** when STT transcript/context is already available and its LiveKit Model License is acceptable. Its published 25 ms / 400 MB figures are modest for 32 GB Macs, but its Python-centric deployment and license make it less clean for a Swift-first distributable app. A silence timer should remain an adjustable accessibility fallback, never the only endpointing signal.

### MVP validation gate

Before locking the recognizer, compare Parakeet and WhisperKit on a small consented set of real tutor sessions spanning accented English, Mexican Spanish, mid-sentence pauses, quiet speech, names/math terms and room noise. Record WER/CER, endpoint false-cutoff and missed-end rates, first partial latency, finalization latency, peak memory while the tutor LLM is loaded, and correction/revision behavior. Make pause threshold, VAD sensitivity, “wait longer” affordance, and push-to-finish controls user-adjustable: accessibility is the product distinction, and users need control over interruption timing.
